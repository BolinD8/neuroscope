The Signal Lab

Move the slider from inhibition toward excitation. Watch the voltage trace and see when a signal travels down the axon.

Balance the input

Start at rest. Slide right to +15 mV to fire the neuron, then slide left to see inhibition.

Reading the response

Excitation makes the membrane voltage less negative. In this example, a 15 mV rise takes the neuron from −70 mV to its −55 mV firing threshold. Once triggered, the spike rises to about +30 mV, falls briefly below rest, and recovers. A stronger input reaches threshold sooner while the spike keeps its height.

The trace shows one illustrative response at the start of the axon. Playback is slowed so you can follow the impulse along the cell.

Seizure first aid

Stay nearby, time the seizure, and clear objects that could cause injury. If the person is lying down, gently roll them onto their side, mouth toward the ground. Keep their mouth clear and let them move freely.

In the U.S., call 911 if a seizure lasts more than five minutes, another follows soon, the person is injured, or breathing or waking is difficult afterward. Also call for a seizure in water, a first seizure, pregnancy, or diabetes with loss of consciousness.

CDC · First aid for seizures
Interactive lab BrainShift
Cognitive flexibility · learning game

BrainShift

Study the creature, read the rule, and choose yes or no. Watch for a new rule every three creatures.

What changed?

A creature can have wings, horns, both, or neither. When the question changes, the feature that mattered a moment ago may become irrelevant. This untimed activity explores rule switching. Its score describes this round of play and has no diagnostic meaning.

Keyboard play: focus the game, then use the left arrow for yes and the right arrow for no.

Make a neuron

Gather paper, colored pencils, scissors, and tape. Ask an adult for help with cutting.

  1. Color the branching dendrites, cell body, axon, and terminals.
  2. Trace the route a signal takes through the cell.
  3. Cut around the outline. Write on the message tag and tape it beside your neuron.

About NeuroScope

A digital museum for anyone curious about the nervous system and the people whose lives it shapes.

Science you can explore

NeuroScope brings science, creativity, and compassion into one learning space. Choose a brain region, combine neural signals, or add a neuron to a piece of art.

The exhibits use simplified models and original illustrations. They support learning and conversation. Health information is general education. Individual medical concerns belong with a qualified healthcare professional.
